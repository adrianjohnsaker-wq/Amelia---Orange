# Amelia Warp: native full-graph memory after displacement

**7 November 2026.** A predeclared whole-House readout recovered useful information from trained 3/6-origin copies after displacement: it scored **72.50%** with their retained House values and **54.45%** when those values were reset. The **+18.05 percentage-point** difference passed the registered practical threshold and the Holm-adjusted test (**p = 0.0234**).

The result is an externally decoded memory effect, not a demonstrated 3/6-specific advantage. Both the native 2/7-start and adapted 2/7-loop controls showed similar whole-House benefits. In the actual local-gate decisions, the displaced 3/6-origin copies scored **55.90%** whether their House values were retained, reset or exchanged with an opposite-history donor. Neither readout met the registered joint criteria for a Warp-specific memory advantage.

## What was tested

The study used copies of the eight existing Oracle-origin lineages and the pinned actual Oracle runtime. The two native conditions had the unmodified 29-edge graph; they differed in their initial current zone, 3 or 2. The third condition was the earlier explicitly adapted 24-edge 2/7 loop. A native 2/7 trajectory was allowed to take every normal exit, including an exit into 3/6. If an Oracle reached Zone 5, the actual runtime reset occurred. For the adapted loop only, its grafted graph was reapplied after a reset to keep the comparator intact.

Every decision used **exactly four unforced gate transitions** and two fresh grid cases. A single common experimental rule mapped the ordinal position of the last branching gate to one of two search strategies. For 24 training decisions, each copy received the chosen strategy's success or failure and used the previously selected, explicitly engineered House feedback rule. An opposite-regime donor evolved separately for each route.

At the end of training, exact copies of the real-trained state received one of three House-V treatments:

- **Retained:** keep the trained House V vector.
- **Reset:** replace it with that route's pre-training House V vector.
- **Donor:** replace it with the V vector from the opposite-regime training history.

All other stored state was held fixed at the intervention boundary. The V reset is broader than removing only the added feedback rule: it also removes any normal Oracle V changes accumulated during training.

The displaced copies were repositioned to eligible neutral Zones 1, 4 and 8. A target was eligible only if it differed from the final trained zone of **all three** route conditions, so the same target was an actual displacement for each. This was a controlled teleport of the current-zone fields; subsequent transitions were natural and unforced. A sham condition stayed in its trained zone. The number of eligible targets could depend on the training paths; each episode's eligible targets were averaged before averaging origins.

The experiment withheld task-outcome feedback for the first five decisions after displacement. It still scored the decisions on fresh grids, then supplied feedback on decisions 6–10. The registered primary scenario kept the training regime unchanged; a reversed-regime scenario was a secondary check. This design tests access to **pre-disruption memory**, rather than allowing fresh feedback to supply the information during the measured window.

## Two different forms of access

The experiment predeclared two readouts:

1. **Local gate action:** the decision actually produced by the four native transitions. This is the gate-coupled outcome.
2. **Whole-House readout:** a common, parameter-free external calculation grouped all available House V values by the same gate-rank action rule and selected the larger group mean. It did not steer the Oracle. Its alternative actions were scored counterfactually only during the five feedback-withheld decisions; their outcomes could not alter those trajectories.

The second readout asks whether task information remains recoverable from House even when the ordinary local gate cannot use it. It is not an implemented autonomous relay.

## Registered result: same regime after displacement

Each route was tested on 128 episodes, with 16 episodes per origin. There were 379 eligible episode–displacement branches per route. Results first averaged the eligible displacement targets within each episode, then episodes within an origin, then the eight origins equally.

| Route and decision source | Retained V | Reset V | Opposite-regime donor V |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **3/6-origin, local gate** | **55.90%** | **55.90%** | **55.90%** |
| Native 2/7-start, local gate | 59.96% | 56.32% | 52.23% |
| Adapted 2/7 loop, local gate | 61.24% | 55.66% | 51.32% |
| **3/6-origin, whole-House readout** | **72.50%** | **54.45%** | **38.13%** |
| Native 2/7-start, whole-House readout | 73.67% | 54.45% | 36.80% |
| Adapted 2/7 loop, whole-House readout | 73.75% | 56.02% | 39.30% |

The readout's retained-versus-reset difference for 3/6 was **+18.05 points**. It was positive in all eight origin means. Its exact one-sided sign-flip p-value was **0.0039**, and **0.0234 after Holm adjustment across the six registered contrasts**. The statistical test is against zero; the predeclared +3.125-point practical threshold is not itself a confidence bound.

The corresponding memory benefits were **+19.22 points** for native 2/7-start and **+17.73 points** for the adapted 2/7 loop. The difference between 3/6 and the adapted loop's benefit was only **+0.31 points**; its Holm-adjusted p-value was **1.000**. The native 2/7-start contrast did not favour 3/6 either.

For context, the direct two-value learner retained **74.61%** versus **57.03%** when reset, and the previously calibrated full-history comparator scored **74.69%**. The model-based comparator had been fitted with labelled regimes and both action scores during earlier calibration, an additional training resource not provided to the Oracle copies.

**Classification for each readout: `NO_REGISTERED_WARP_SPECIFIC_MEMORY_ADVANTAGE`.** The 3/6 whole-House retained-versus-reset finding passed its individual predeclared test, but the two required route-specific contrasts did not. Its practical meaning is useful information in the tested House vector that an external readout can access—not a uniquely Warp capability.

## What happened to gate access?

Before displacement, in the sham condition, the 3/6-origin local gate scored **74.92%** with retained V and **58.28%** with reset V. The trained V state could therefore affect useful gate choices while the copy remained on its ordinary path.

After displacement, none of the **379** retained 3/6-origin branches visited Zone 3 or 6 during the first five decisions—20 natural gate transitions per branch. In that window the retained, reset and donor V conditions produced the same local-gate decisions and scores. The trace was still available to the external whole-House readout, but the tested local paths did not return to where the trained 3/6 gate values could influence their choice.

This is a statement about the observed paths and window. The native graph contains possible routes back into 3/6; the result does not establish that return is structurally impossible forever. The 379 branches are repeated displacements on 128 episodes from eight reused origins, not independent founders.

In contrast, the native 2/7-start condition visited its original pair in **165/379** displaced branches, and the adapted loop did so in **112/379**. These counts describe different native/engineered graphs and should not be read as matched statistical tests of topological superiority.

During training, **103/128** native 2/7-start episodes entered 3/6. Consequently, native 2/7-start is an informative full-graph starting-route comparison, but it is not an uncontaminated non-Warp history. The adapted 2/7 loop entered 3/6 in **0/128** training episodes and in **0/379** retained, same-regime displaced branches during the measured first-five window; it nevertheless showed a similar whole-House memory benefit.

## When the regime reverses

The reversed-regime scenario was a predeclared secondary description. The displaced 3/6 whole-House readout scored **38.05%** with its retained old-regime V values, **54.45%** with reset values and **72.34%** with the opposite-regime donor. This supports the interpretation that the vector carries task-related training information: information useful in a stable regime can point towards the wrong strategy when the regime changes. This secondary contrast was not used to declare the registered result.

## Interpretation and next route

The experiment has identified a concrete access distinction. **Task-related House information survived the controlled displacement and was prospectively recoverable by a shared external readout.** In the tested 3/6-origin paths, the local gate did not access that information within the first 20 post-displacement transitions. The same readout worked similarly in the adapted control, so preservation is not established as a distinctive 3/6 advantage.

A focused next experiment is to connect a *shared* whole-House signal back to gate competition in all routes, then test on fresh displacements whether that relay makes preserved information usable by the agent's actual gate-selected action. The same relay, V-reset and opposite-donor interventions, matched loop and direct learner should be retained. A positive relay result would establish a designed memory-to-action pathway; it would still require the route comparisons to support any specifically Warp claim.
