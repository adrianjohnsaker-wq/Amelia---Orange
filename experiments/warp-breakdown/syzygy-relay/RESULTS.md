# Amelia: Numogram syzygy relay and action recovery

**8 November 2026.** The experiment created a working, explicitly engineered route from retained 3/6 House values to Amelia's actual gate-selected actions after displacement. On the stable-regime task, the 3/6 focused relay recovered the previously calibrated preferred action in **85.94%** of weighted cases, versus **50.00%** with its House values reset. The retained-versus-reset contrast was **+35.94 percentage points**, Holm-adjusted **p = 0.0391**. Exchanging opposite-regime values also changed recovery, by **+71.88 points** for retained versus donor, adjusted **p = 0.0391**.

This is an access result for the designed memory-to-gate pathway. It is **not a registered 3/6-specific advantage**: the matched 2/7 relay recovered in **85.94%**, exactly the same as 3/6; the 3/6 generic relay recovered in **84.90%**. In a post-outcome trace check, the 3/6 focused phase relay, matched 2/7 focused phase relay, 3/6 no-phase focused relay, 3/6 1/8-phase control and direct learner made identical first-five action sequences on all **188/188** matched displacement branches. That does not mean their underlying gate paths or later actions were identical.

A separate engineered edge forced 3/6 entry and improved the registered recovery measure relative to its neutral sham: **89.06%** versus **45.83%**, difference **+43.23 points**, adjusted **p = 0.0391**. The corresponding advantage over the adapted 2/7 entry-minus-sham contrast was not established, and the engineered 3/6 entry did not clear the registered threshold against the generic relay. Consequently the entry-specific class is also negative. A temporary added edge is a designed intervention, not evidence of an intrinsic Numogram shortcut.

## What the experiment measures

Each of the eight existing Oracle-origin lineages supplied eight fresh synthetic episodes: four paired seeds, each used in both complementary starting regimes. After 24 training decisions, copies were displaced to eligible neutral zones. There were **64 episodes and 188 episode–displacement branches per route**. Conditions for the same route and displacement started as clones; route preparations and training followed the predeclared native or adapted graph. The first five outcomes after displacement were withheld, and outcome feedback resumed after decision six. The stable training regime is the registered primary condition; an immediate regime reversal is a secondary condition. No world-stream input or live Oracle state was changed.

The outcome `recovery` means **two consecutive gate-selected choices of the strategy previously calibrated as best on average in that regime**, within the first five decisions. That calibrated label is fixed in advance and not supplied to the controller. It does not assert that the strategy is optimal for each individual grid. Each decision takes four scored gate events. The separate grid column is the actual fraction of two synthetic grid cases solved at each of those first five decisions.

Restricted mean latency counts four transitions per decision and caps non-recovery at 20. A success on the fifth decision and a censored case both contribute 20, so the recovery percentage is essential alongside the mean. Direct learners do not traverse the Numogram and have no comparable gate count. Values first average displacements within episodes, episodes within lineages, and then the eight lineages equally.

The 3/6 relay versus natural-gate recovery difference is **+29.69 points** and the restricted latency is **6.11 transitions** lower descriptively. Its predeclared recovery contrast has adjusted **p = 0.0547**, just above 0.05, so it is not a registered statistically significant relay-versus-natural comparison. The latency difference was descriptive, not a separately registered significance test. The full-history model had access to labelled regimes and counterfactual action outcomes in earlier calibration; this extra prior information makes it a useful strong comparator, not an identical-resource control. The native 2/7-start route entered Zones 3/6 during training in 50/64 episodes; the adapted 2/7 control did so in 0/64, making the latter the cleaner non-Warp training comparison.

## Memory intervention

Only the specified House V vector was retained, reset to the same route's pretraining state, or exchanged for the opposite-regime donor at the intervention boundary; normalized hashes of the rest of the state were checked. The relay then made decisions without the regime label.

| 3/6 focused phase relay | Recovered | Restricted mean transitions | Grids solved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3/6 focused phase relay: retain | 85.94% | 9.69 | 75.47% |
| 3/6 focused phase relay: reset | 50.00% | 14.25 | 53.96% |
| 3/6 focused phase relay: donor | 14.06% | 18.31 | 33.59% |

In the first 20 transitions, the remote 3/6 relay physically returned to Zones 3 or 6 in **0/188** displaced branches. It still delivered the House-derived scalar at each gate evaluation. This shows a functioning *engineered nonlocal access channel* in the tested copies; it does not show that the native graph traversed an edge into the Warp. Over the full first-five window there were **3,760 scalar deliveries and 3,760 bounded phase pulses** across those 188 branches. These are extra information and update resources, counted separately from the 3,760 native gate transitions. The runtime also computed additional House summaries for logging; actual processor work is not inferred from the logical signal count.

## Physical entry and observed traversal

In the 3/6 entry arm, the first post-displacement event was a forced, temporary edge into Zone 3; **188/188** branches entered on that event by construction. The augmented event counted as one of the same four gate events per decision. For the stable first-five window this meant **188 augmented and 3,572 unaugmented events**; the corresponding sham forced a temporary edge to a neutral zone. All 188 3/6 entry endpoints were absent from the native shock-zone exits. In adapted 2/7, **126/188** forced endpoints were newly introduced and **62/188** already existed; in the latter cases the runtime recorded only the destination and could not distinguish the duplicate temporary edge from the native one. This difference matters when interpreting the entry comparison.

The earlier result of **0/379 observed returns within 20 gate transitions** described selected paths, not graph-theoretic distance. With the runtime's Zone-5 reset semantics, shortest native directed paths from displacement Zones 1, 4 and 8 to {3,6} have lengths **2, 3 and 3**. For example, 1→2→3 is two events. The corresponding shortest distances to {2,7} are **1, 1 and 1**. A path being topologically available does not mean the gate-scoring runtime selects it.

## Registered comparisons

Each contrast is the equally weighted mean of eight origin-paired recovery differences. Ten exact one-sided sign-flip p-values were adjusted together by Holm. A contrast passes only with a mean difference of at least +5 percentage points *and* adjusted p ≤ 0.05. The p-values test zero, not whether the five-point threshold is exceeded. Both overall classifications require every comparison in their respective family to pass.

## Exploratory reversal lead

The following time course was examined **after** the registered outcome; it is descriptive and was not an independently sealed recovery test. The regime was reversed at displacement. No new outcome was supplied for the first five decisions; the first new feedback is delivered *after* decision 6 and can affect decision 7. Each entry is the percentage choosing the newly calibrated preferred strategy at that particular decision, **not** the two-consecutive-action recovery metric.

| Decision after displacement | 3/6 remote focused relay | 3/6 engineered entry | 3/6 generic relay | Adapted 2/7 engineered entry | Direct learner |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 14.06% | 15.62% | 16.67% | 13.02% | 14.06% |
| 6 | 14.06% | 15.62% | 16.67% | 10.94% | 14.06% |
| 7 | 15.62% | 46.88% | 47.40% | 46.88% | 48.44% |
| 8 | 20.31% | 62.50% | 63.80% | 62.50% | 64.06% |
| 9 | 19.01% | 71.88% | 75.52% | 73.44% | 75.00% |
| 10 | 25.00% | 85.94% | 87.24% | 85.94% | 87.50% |

At decisions 6–10, actual feedback updates in the 3/6 entry arm were applied at a 3/6 source in **188/188 branches at each decision**. In the 3/6 remote-focused arm, those counts were **0, 0, 2, 2 and 2 out of 188**, respectively; its feedback was mostly applied elsewhere. These repeated branch counts are not independent subjects. Together with the time course, this suggests a testable distinction between remote *reading* of retained 3/6 memory and restoring a route through which current feedback can *update* the relevant House values. It does not by itself establish causation, and matched 2/7 entry and the generic relay also adapted in this setting.

A focused follow-up would give both 3/6 and matched 2/7 the same bidirectional House-to-gate relay, compare remote reading alone against a feedback-writing connection and engineered entry, and seal a reversal-recovery endpoint after feedback resumes. This directly tests whether a circuit can turn breakdown into an updated action policy and whether any benefit is specific to 3/6.
