# Cybergothic assemblage: developing the four mechanisms

**14 November 2026 · separate Python research prototype · four fresh-seed studies**

This work develops the earlier reactor into a changing ecology in which the four requested mechanisms act on one another. Corpses retain fragments of lived performance; viral packets carry executable fragments between hosts; temporal commitments orient a host toward a possible coming environment; and an overloaded agent's remaining energy can return to the necrotic reactor. The result is still a simulation, but the tests now ask how these processes change resource use, recovery, anticipation and the distribution of energy, rather than counting new strings alone.

The strongest results were specific. Selecting viral cargo from a donor's experienced code increased the population's total resource uptake against a pointer-selected cargo control over a fixed period, although a subsequent fixed-action comparison did not show a clear advantage. A fading temporal trace adapted much more quickly to a reversal in the generated environment, at some cost before the reversal. Meltdown salvage displaced external reactor input. A portable prediction claim increased the energy taken by claimants, mainly by transferring it from other hosts; it did not clearly increase population-wide nutrient uptake. A more selective archive performed **worse** than random archive selection after total population loss in this particular environment.

The prototype and tests remain separate from Amelia Orange. No live Amelia state, gate, Governor, memory, database or scheduled experiment was modified.

## The assemblage

The 29 directed Numogram gates, Θ = 12 transient flight, asynchronous agent schedules, persistent corpses, homoiconic digit strings and 45 mutable matrix cells are inherited from the prior reactor. The CCRU Warp is 3/6; the ecology's 7–9 mutation furnace is a modeling role. The matrix's demon-class mapping remains experimental.

A new environment maintains finite, competing resources in three channels at each zone. Independently scheduled pulses change which channel is replenished. The same digit an agent executes through the matrix also influences its embodied resource focus: it may hold, rotate, sense at a cost, or recall a productive focus. Digit order and the instruction pointer therefore affect what the host can obtain. Resource stocks, exogenous input, uptake, forecasts, packets and source ancestry are logged.

| Component | Developed mechanism | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Necrotic recovery | A dead host's archive includes its code, ancestry and prior channel-specific uptake. Recovery can sample randomly, rank past performance or select a portfolio with respect to present local resources and living hosts. | Reconstitute energy-costed hosts from retained material after the entire population dies. |
| Viral ecology | A direct infection can shed a code packet that travels through existing gates. Later packets can carry fragments that were productive in the donor's experience. | Insert executable fragments at a recipient's instruction pointer, potentially continuing after the donor moves or dies. |
| Temporal attractor | An agent commits a costly forecast of the next environmental regime using observed transitions. A retained orientation can influence subsequent forage actions. | Use a possible future to organize present commitments, then act on the committed orientation after the environment reveals itself. |
| Runaway feedback | At meltdown, some remaining virtual energy is moved into a bounded Cthell vault. The vault can subsequently supply the reactor. | Turn part of a destructive event into an accounted input for later activity. |

The environment is generated by a private pseudorandom process with deliberately correlated transitions. Its direction reverses during each observation period. Agents can use signals already revealed to the simulation but the forecast routine does not read the future RNG. This is a designed test of anticipation and adaptation within the model.

## Viral code and living performance

After a common warm-up and a shared partial-population shock, 30 fresh matched populations ran for 180 model-time units per arm. In the advanced arm, a donor could select an executable fragment of up to four digits that had previously coincided with productive forage, provided that fragment still existed in its genome. The pointer control used the fragment at the donor's current instruction pointer. Both used the same vector cost, gate rules, field conditions and remaining mechanisms.

| Outcome | Experienced fragment | Pointer fragment |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mean total nutrient harvested | 136.1 | 131.0 |
| Mean agent-time | 5,609 | 5,001 |
| Mean uptake per executed action | 0.0398 | 0.0414 |
| Vector infections across 30 runs | 3,565 | 3,037 |

The paired increase in total nutrient uptake was **5.16 virtual units** (99.17% family-adjusted bootstrap interval **0.64 to 9.60**), positive in 23/30 seeds. This comparison supports a difference in the whole modeled ecology following the intervention; the populations and numbers of later opportunities diverged. It does **not** show that each recipient became more efficient.

That qualification matters. In a post-outcome sensitivity analysis, all 30 experienced-fragment and pointer branches reached 1,500 subsequent actions. At that common action budget, uptake differed by **+0.65** units (unadjusted 95% interval **−2.00 to +3.59**). Mean per-action uptake over the full horizon was also slightly lower in the experienced arm. The total-time advantage is therefore compatible with a larger or more active ecology rather than a demonstrated per-action benefit.

A control that shuffled the experienced fragment's digit order while preserving its digit and ancestry multiset did not clearly differ from intact experienced fragments: the paired total-uptake difference was **+2.82** (99.17% interval **−1.15 to +7.67**). Among 3,265 observed next host executions after a vector infection in the experienced arm, 2,555 executed the inserted first digit. That verifies a route into behavior, but the study does not yet establish that the particular copied order is responsible for the population-level difference.

The first study had compared travelling vectors with no additional travelling vectors, keeping ordinary direct infection and splicing active. Its matched uptake difference was **+0.12** (99.17% interval **−5.74 to +6.08**). Thus the second study's experienced-versus-pointer result should not be recast as a demonstrated advantage over having no travelling vectors at all.

## Necrotic memory and recovery

In a separate matched set of branches, every living agent was killed. The active corpse bank and persistent archive survived, except in an empty-bank control. Recovery used an eight-host reseeding threshold and charged four virtual energy units for each archive birth; ordinary fission could increase the population further. All archive policies had the same initial resources and renewal rules.

| Recovery policy | Mean total nutrient harvested | Mean living agents at horizon | Archive births / natural fissions, all 30 runs |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Random archive sample | 115.3 | 15.1 | 360 / 987 |
| Experience-and-coverage portfolio | 103.6 | 12.1 | 364 / 863 |
| Portfolio with recalled order shuffled | 106.2 | 14.2 | 383 / 914 |
| Empty active bank and archive | 0 | 0 | 0 / 0 |

Portfolio minus random uptake was **−11.70** (99.17% interval **−20.57 to −3.56**), negative in 22/30 paired seeds. The portfolio was intended to recover complementary prior capabilities, but this implementation did not outperform random sampling in the changing environment. Portfolio versus its order-shuffled control was **−2.55** (99.17% interval **−14.95 to +11.96**), so the effect of recalled order remains unresolved. In the preceding study, ranking corpses solely by past average uptake also failed to separate clearly from random selection. These outcomes favor retaining random archive selection as the current default for the tested recovery setting; they do not establish that memory-guided recovery is generally ineffective.

The live research version has a real transactional SQLite checkpoint. In one additional demonstration, all living agents were killed with 111 archive records retained; the checkpoint was loaded in a new Python process, ran for 90 further model-time units and ended with three living agents after 13 archive reconstitutions. Its complete event history and state matched an uninterrupted continuation. Agent births from the archive and ordinary fission remain distinct in the ledger.

## Temporal anticipation and the reversal

The first fresh-seed study compared the learned next-regime prediction with repeating the last observed signal on the **same completed forecast windows**. The learned prediction's mean advantage was **18.65 percentage points** (99.17% interval **16.44 to 20.77**). However, regeneration of all 30 advanced branches showed that this advantage was concentrated before the environment reversed: it fell from about **31.8 points before** to **2.0 points after** the reversal (exploratory 95% post-reversal interval −0.1 to +4.3).

The response was to let the temporal trace gradually lose weight. A fresh 30-seed study evaluated three traces on the same environmental sequence in every zone, using only observations available before each reveal. Each phase contained 4,500 shadow predictions across the 30 seeds. The living ecology also used the trace, but populations and opportunities could diverge there.

| Trace retention per environmental update | Accuracy before reversal | Accuracy after reversal |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1.00: retain all observations | 50.1% | 20.8% |
| 0.85: moderate fading | 48.2% | 37.5% |
| 0.70: faster fading | 45.0% | 41.4% |

After reversal, moderate fading exceeded full retention by **16.7 percentage points** (97.5% adjusted interval **15.0 to 18.3**); faster fading exceeded it by **20.6 points** (97.5% interval **18.6 to 22.5**). Both comparisons were positive in all 30 seeds. Fading sacrificed some accuracy in the earlier stable regime, illustrating a memory/adaptation trade-off. The result concerns this deliberately structured virtual environment, not a physically unknown future.

The living-ecology comparison is separate. In the second study, learned orientation increased total uptake by **3.78** units over the last-signal orientation (99.17% interval **0.48 to 7.22**). In the later fading-trace study, moderate fading increased uptake by **2.17** units relative to accumulated memory (exploratory, unadjusted 95% interval **0.52 to 3.88**); its agent-time difference was not clearly separated from zero. These whole-population results can reflect changes in population size and opportunities as well as forecasting.

## Portable claims and predatory feedback

The new environmental forecast initially paid a commitment cost but often lost its reward if its living owner moved out of the zone before resolution. A follow-up kept the claim attached to a surviving owner. If the committed environmental target arrived, the claim could collect a capped field payment from its original zone even after the owner had moved. In **both** local and portable arms, an eligible correct claim could also levy a small amount from each other resident there, subject to a cap. The field and the other hosts were debited; this is a transfer within the simulation.

Thirty new matched seeds compared the two policies after the same shock. Net claim energy here means total rewards minus commitment costs; it excludes the separate cost of action orientation and is not a lifetime return for an individual.

| Outcome per run, mean | Local claim | Portable claim |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Claim rewards | 10.00 | 26.68 |
| Commitment costs | 10.23 | 10.41 |
| Net claim energy | −0.23 | +16.27 |
| Energy levied from residents | 9.61 | 24.17 |
| Distinct levied hosts | 46.2 | 65.6 |
| Total nutrient harvested | 131.32 | 130.94 |

Portable minus local net claim energy was **+16.50** (97.5% adjusted interval **12.87 to 20.83**), positive in all 30 seeds. Total nutrient uptake differed by **−0.38** (97.5% interval **−3.75 to +3.12**); agent-time also did not clearly separate in an exploratory comparison. The claimants' increased return is largely redistribution from other agents, not demonstrated extra energy for the population. The study does not yet establish the effect on the longer-term survival of the levied hosts.

The Ampy vault is a separate mechanism. In the second study, salvage-on branches received a mean **7.81** units of external reactor renewal, compared with **29.30** without salvage. The paired reduction was **21.49** (99.17% interval **18.41 to 24.52**). Salvage-on branches collected an average of **35.41** units from dying agents' remaining energy and released **24.89** from the vault into the reactor. The amount of subsequent demand and the number of archive births differed between trajectories; reduced external input is not by itself a proof of better population viability. The first study's salvage-on versus salvage-off agent-time contrast was not clearly separated from zero.

## Design, verification and next question

Four successive studies used 30 fresh seeds each, yielding **840 matched-branch trajectories**: the first developed assemblage, the experienced-code/portfolio revision, temporal fading, and portable claims. The plans and code hashes were saved before the outcomes of each fresh-seed set. Development used separate inspected pilot seeds; these are locally fixed research pilots, not independent public registrations. The two six-contrast studies report 99.17% Bonferroni-adjusted intervals; the fading-trace and portable-claim studies report 97.5% intervals for their two planned contrasts. Later action-budget and regime analyses are marked exploratory.

The final runnable version passed **21 tests**, including ordered-genome behavior, vector insertion, future commitment before reveal, independent environmental RNG, archive recovery, transfer conservation, process restart, and locally simulated cloud contracts. Three event ledgers per study were regenerated against their stored state and event hashes. The temporal shadow scores were independently reconstructed from the environmental histories for all 30 fading-trace seeds. The evidence package includes per-seed results, the selected full ledgers, pilot records and a usable SQLite recovery checkpoint.

The Supabase-compatible adapter preserves the expanded virtual state in a local HTTP-contract test. It has **not** been verified against a separate hosted Supabase project, its database policies or its production API. The tested durable store for these experiments is SQLite; Amelia's existing Supabase memory remains untouched.

The next informative experiment is a host-level randomized transfer assay. At the same eligible encounter, allocate experienced, pointer-selected or shuffled fragments to comparable recipients, follow the same post-transfer action and survival window, and keep the packet's energy and route fixed. That would test whether a specific code fragment changes a host's behavior under comparable exposure. A complementary recovery assay should vary the environment's recurrence and novelty while matching archive-birth and energy budgets; the present archive result shows that selection based on prior success can be a poor guide in a changed milieu.
