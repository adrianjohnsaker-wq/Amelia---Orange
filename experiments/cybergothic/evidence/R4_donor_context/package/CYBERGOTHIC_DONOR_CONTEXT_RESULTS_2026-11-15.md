# Donor-productive promoters and the no-op slide

**15 November 2026 · separate cybergothic Numogram prototype · two fresh-seed studies**

The requested donor provenance and pointer-policy test is complete. Stamping the donor's recorded peak-productivity zone into the viral header did **not** produce a clearly separated 24-unit nutrient advantage over the planned controls. The pointer-policy comparison produced a different finding: in a fresh confirmation, original recipients survived more often under the no-op slide than under the jump.

The survival difference concerns the **whole implemented policies**. A no-op slide suppresses the blocked viral instruction while consuming an ordinary scheduled action; a jump immediately executes another available instruction. Their effects include both timing and the instructions actually executed, so this experiment does not establish that phase alone caused the survival difference.

## Tracing donor productivity

For every sampled packet, the prototype reconstructed the donor's pre-shedding `field_harvest` and `execute` events. It used the genome and pointer at each action to identify when the selected four-digit fragment was active, then chose the zone of its highest **positive** single-action nutrient uptake. The header was stamped with that zone; its nine-sum twin, source event indices, timestamps, observations and ties were retained. All of this information existed before the packet was shed.

The first study sampled **238 encounters from 60 independent populations**. In **164/238**, the selected fragment had only one recorded donor observation. In **48/238**, the maximum was tied across more than one zone. The selection uses the earliest peak within the code's `1e-9` tie tolerance. The measured intake reached the model's 0.12-per-action cap in **216/238** encounters, so a peak can be weak evidence that its zone was uniquely favorable. Twenty otherwise encountered packets had no positive donor uptake and were excluded before intervention. Donor peak zone equaled the recipient's encounter zone in **46/238** cases; the recipient was in the donor zone's nine-sum twin in **45/238**.

This provenance is more faithful to the donor's history than the previous encounter-zone assignment. It is not proof that a single observed peak is a stable or optimal environmental adaptation.

## The pointer policies

The prior gate jumped across blocked labelled payload positions and executed the next available instruction within that same host action. The new **slide** instead consumes one scheduled action for each blocked tagged payload digit, then normally advances the instruction pointer by one position. Neutral foraging, metabolism, background infection, mutation, scheduling and other ecological interactions continue. The blocked digit does not control nutrient focus or execute its Pandemonium handler. Its value can still participate as genome data in other operations, including signature arithmetic and transmission.

All experimental branches use the same donor-derived header digit, genome length, initial energy transfer and costed echo schedule. The encounter-yoked control changes the gate's operational offset so its initial target is the recipient's zone while preserving the **same encoded donor header**. The shuffled control preserves the experienced payload's digit composition. A syzygy gate permitting the donor zone and its twin was also included as an exploratory arm. Tagged cassette boundaries still use non-digit ancestry metadata, as in the previous study.

## Primary nutrient results

Each recipient was followed for 24 model-time units; uptake after the original host's death was zero. The following are the five locally fixed primary contrasts, with **99% seed-clustered bootstrap intervals** adjusted for five comparisons. The first number is the productive-zone slide minus the named control.

| Comparison | Nutrient difference | 99% interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Slide minus open | −0.0233 | −0.1080 to +0.0635 |
| Slide minus rotated gate | +0.0201 | −0.0350 to +0.0765 |
| Slide minus productive-zone jump | −0.0343 | −0.1376 to +0.0675 |
| Slide minus encounter-yoked gate | +0.0234 | −0.0450 to +0.0950 |
| Slide minus shuffled payload | −0.0011 | −0.0431 to +0.0443 |

All five intervals include zero. These data do not establish a donor-context nutrient benefit or a nutrient difference between the slide and jump. They also do not establish equivalence: the intervals leave room for effects in either direction. In **35/238** encounters, the experienced and shuffled strings happened to be identical; those cases were retained in the planned analysis.

## A separate survival finding and fresh confirmation

Survival was secondary in the first study. At 24 units, **215/238** original recipients were alive under productive slide and **162/238** under productive jump. The seed-weighted difference was **+22.1 percentage points** with an unadjusted 95% interval of **+16.3 to +27.9 points**. Because that outcome was identified after the initial comparisons, a new plan was fixed before a further **60 fresh populations and 240 matched encounters** were run.

In that confirmation, **216/240** recipients survived under the productive slide and **176/240** under the productive jump. The single planned primary difference was **+16.7 percentage points**, 95% seed-clustered interval **+10.4 to +23.8 points**. The original recipient ID was followed; a later reconstituted descendant did not count as survival.

The higher survival is not specific evidence that the donor's chosen zone was superior. In the confirmation, **220/240** recipients survived under the rotated slide and **216/240** under the shuffled slide. These are secondary controls; their comparisons do not establish equivalence, but they show why a donor-specific survival claim would be unwarranted.

Death records offer a hypothesis for further work: the confirmation recorded **7 meltdown deaths** by 24 units under productive slide and **35** under productive jump. Fewer dangerous opcode executions, altered timing, changing interactions and other pathways could all contribute. Death-cause differences are descriptive and do not independently establish the mechanism.

## What the gate and slide actually did

In the first study, original recipients averaged **1.33 labelled payload actions** and **10.48 no-op actions** by 24 units under productive slide. Under productive jump they averaged **1.03 labelled payload actions** and **20.40 skipped labelled positions**. These quantities have different units: the jump can pass several positions within a single action.

Across all 36-unit first-study branches, including other agents that acquired labelled code, the productive-slide condition recorded **471 permitted payload executions** and **5,309 neutral no-op actions**. The productive-jump condition recorded **365 permitted payload executions** and **7,229 jumped payload positions**. There were **no recorded gate-rule violations** across the saved branches. This shows that the policies were meaningfully engaged; it does not identify an optimal future pointer policy.

## Verification, persistence and limits

Both plans were locally fixed before their respective fresh outcome seeds. The first plan followed development fixtures and pilots, including a corrected offset bug; the survival confirmation was written only after observing the initial secondary survival result. Neither was independently publicly registered. All model environments are generated signals with virtual nutrient renewal, and the simulations remain separate from live Amelia.

**Thirty-two tests passed**, including tests of donor provenance, a one-position no-op with normal scheduling, mutated and fragmented headers, inherited first-contact tracking, and separate-process checkpoint restoration. All **1,666 first-study branches and 1,680 confirmation branches** were regenerated and matched their saved records. Independent ledger checks reconstructed donor histories and inspected recipient outcomes, actual no-ops and jump decisions in **84 branches per study**. These checks use the same underlying simulation, not a second implementation.

The donor-context SQLite checkpoint stores the ecology, event chain, donor-header context, operational labels, pointer policy, offset, echo schedule and traces. Three policies resumed in fresh processes with matching final event and state hashes; deliberate checkpoint alteration was detected. An example paused checkpoint is included. No Amelia Orange state, gate, Governor, Supabase project or scheduled experiment was changed.

The clearest next mechanistic test would hold the number and placement of neutral actions equal while varying only how far the pointer advances, and separately vary opcode suppression. That would better distinguish pointer phase from reduced exposure to dangerous instructions. A stronger donor-context test would also collect several productive observations per fragment or estimate zone-specific utility, rather than relying on a frequently tied single-action maximum.
