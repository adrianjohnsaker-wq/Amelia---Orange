## What the three field studies show together (ND-1c, ND-1d, ND-1e)
1. **The faintest order is at the order test's floor for every architecture.**
   - **Detection over all sixteen 2% streams** (ND-1d stage 1, ND-1d stage 2 and ND-1e): D10 8 of 16, today's architecture 6 of 16.
   - **Which streams.** The two catch different streams and agree on only one of the eight here (p07).
   - **What follows.** Detection at 2% is chance-like for both, so it does not separate the architectures.
2. **On exact sequence, the field designs now match or beat today's architecture.**
   - D10's 1-8-7-2 read-back was at or above today's at every rate here.
   - The 1-8-9-1 blur of ND-1 is gone in every field design since ND-1b.
3. **Lowering the field weight costs the current.**
   - At field weight 1.0 the current reading was supported on h09 but not on h10.
   - The field is what carries the current, as ND-1 showed.
   - ND-1b (field weight 2.0) supported all six on h06.

## Reading
- **D10 is not an improvement on ND-1b.** It trades the current for read-back, and its 2% detection is not reliably better.
- **ND-1b remains the app's runtime.** The 2% gap that started this line of work turns out to be at the order test's detection
  floor for every architecture, rather than a defect of the field.
- **Where to look next.** A sharper sequence instrument (longer segments or more shuffles at the faintest rates) would help
  more than further retuning of the field.
